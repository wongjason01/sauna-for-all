The Public Sauna-Bathing Charter

Ten principles for public sauna as common good

The Charter sets out shared principles for developing and caring for public sauna responsibly. It gives communities, operators, governments, funders, and researchers a common reference point. Signatories commit to showing how these principles guide their decisions and daily practice.

Ten shared principles

01–03 · Practice– Foundational values
01

Access for All

Public sauna-bathing is for all people. Access means a door everyone can afford to walk through, a building every body can use, a culture where all people are welcomed and safe, and locations communities can actually reach.

02

Shared Heat, Shared Space

Built on equality and presence, the public sauna-bath is a shared civic place, promoting the health of people and the living world. Community takes work: everyone who enters helps tend it, and is supported to do so.

03

Honour Tradition

We honour and support the many cultures and knowledge holders who keep sauna and sweat-bathing alive, entering each tradition as guests.

04–06 · Keeping– Run for public good
04

Public Good Before Private Gain

Whatever the ownership model, a public sauna-bath is run for the public good. Profit may serve that mission, but never override it.

05

A Sense of Place

At its fullest, a public sauna-bath is a community sauna-bath, shaped by its place: in design, story and benefit. It is built with care for where it stands, carrying both the traditions it comes from and the stories of where it now lives, and keeping value in community rather than extracting it.

06

Community First

A community sauna-bath answers to its community, shaped by the hands and voices of the people it serves. Visitors are welcomed as guests, in ways that sustain the place rather than strain it.

07–08 · Tending– Ongoing care
07

Tended With Care

Built to evidence-informed best practice, maintained for safety, and operated on safer-space principles, a public sauna-bath is stewarded responsibly, so people are treated with dignity and protected from harm.

08

Reciprocity with the Natural World

Public sauna-bathing lives in reciprocity with the natural world that sustains it: powered, built and run to tread lightly, giving back more than taking. It draws people into deepening relationship with the living world, with reverence for the peoples who have long bathed in balance with their surroundings.

09–10 · Stewardship– Shared futures
09

Wellbeing That Circulates

Public sauna-bathing builds relational well-being, spreading care outward: for body, mind and spirit, for the bonds between people, for healthy, regenerative local economies. What we learn, we share across borders, building a common body of practice.

10

Recognised and Resourced

Resilient social infrastructure, built responsibly today so communities can thrive for generations to come, deserves recognition and support: from local and regional government in planning, regulation and funding, and from investors who value it as a public good, not a private bet.

Who can sign

Built by, and for, everyone shaping sauna in their communities

Anyone working to strengthen public sauna as a common good can sign. You don’t need to run a sauna. You simply state what you will do, in your own role, to support the Charter’s principles.

Those who build and run public sauna. Operators and keepers, community sauna organisations and networks, designers, builders, and suppliers.

Those who shape the conditions for it. Governments and public bodies, funders and investors, researchers and educators.

Those who use and champion it. Bathers and advocates.

How signing works

Four steps to joining the movement

Signing is free and takes about 15 to 30 minutes. You choose the principles closest to your work and say, in your own words, how you will put them into practice.

1. Read the Charter

Get to know the ten principles and talk them through with your team, board, or council.

2. Find a reference

Ask an existing signatory, a person or an organisation, for a few words of support. We can help if you don’t know anyone yet.

3. Complete the questionnaire

The form has three parts: Commitment, Contribution, and Consent and Affirmation.

4. Welcome

The founding stewards review applications together, two to four times a year. Once confirmed, you join the signatories and the wider network, and we check in on your commitments as your work grows.

What the Charter is
  • A shared set of principles. A common basis for developing and caring for public sauna responsibly.
  • A common reference point. For communities, operators, governments, funders, researchers, and industry.
  • A guide. For planning, governance, operations, investment, and policy.
  • A commitment. To public sauna that is safe, accessible, culturally respectful, and trustworthy.
  • A foundation. For shared guidance, resources, and learning across the movement.
What the Charter isn’t
  • A certification. It does not certify, audit, or rank public saunas.
  • A rulebook or technical standard. It sets out shared principles, and leaves technical detail to local codes and expertise.
  • A replacement for regulation. It works alongside existing laws and safety requirements.
  • A single model for everyone. It respects diverse cultures, traditions, and local contexts.
  • Fixed in place. Its principles are written to endure, and any revisions are rare, considered, and made in the open.
